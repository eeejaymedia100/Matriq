# Matriq — Logo Design Brief

Written to work either as a creative brief for a designer or as a prompt for an AI image tool. The reference image supplied is a style reference only — glossy finish, dimensional rendering, rounded-square container, gradient, rim glow. The actual mark inside it needs to be original to Matriq, not a re-skin of that logo's specific shape.

---

## Recommended direction: an angular "M" monogram

The mockups already in circulation use "M Matriq" as the wordmark — the icon should be a distilled, abstracted version of that same M, not a different symbol competing with it. This is also the safer, more timeless choice for an app icon: single-letter or simple-geometric marks are what actually stay legible at 24px in a search result or a notification tray, long after more illustrative ideas stop reading clearly.

**Construction:** an angular, faceted M — built from clean diagonal cuts rather than soft rounded strokes, echoing the faceted, gem-like construction of the reference logo. Not a standard typeface M redrawn; treat it as a geometric symbol first, a letterform second. Two or three flat facets per stroke, catching light at different angles, is enough — avoid over-complicating it into something that turns to mud at small sizes.

**Alternate direction, worth a parallel exploration:** a faceted spark/node mark instead of a letterform — a four-point spark (the same signature shape already reserved in the app's UI for anything AI-touched) or a small angular node-and-connection motif suggesting intelligence/network. This has a real advantage: it could double as both the app icon and the in-app "AI-touched" signature icon, creating one consistent mark across brand and product instead of two unrelated symbols. Worth mocking up alongside the M and comparing — this brief leads with the M as the safer default, not because the spark idea is weaker.

---

## Material & rendering

Match the reference image's finish, not its shape: a glossy, semi-translucent, dimensional surface — like cut glass or polished gemstone, not a flat vector fill. Light should catch one edge of the mark as a bright highlight while the opposite edge falls into shadow, giving it real dimensionality rather than a flat gradient overlay. A soft glow bleeds outward from behind the icon into the background, the same way the reference does.

---

## Color

Pull directly from Matriq's established tokens — don't introduce new brand colors here:
- Gradient running from deep purple (`#55278F` / `#7B4BC4`) through to electric lime (`#C6FF3D`) at the highlight edge, the same pairing already anchoring both Glass and Pop themes.
- Background/container: near-black deep purple (`#14061F`), consistent with the Glass theme's base.
- The lime should read as the "light source" — where it appears, it should look like the brightest, most catching part of the mark, echoing lime's existing role in the product as the "this is alive" accent color.

---

## Container & format

- Rounded-square container (a "squircle," not a plain rounded rectangle) — iOS-style continuous corner curvature, roughly 22% corner radius relative to the icon's width, since that's the convention most platforms and app stores expect.
- Design the mark inside the safe zone Android adaptive icons require (roughly the center 66% of the canvas), since the outer edges get cropped differently across launchers.
- Must hold up at three sizes without redesign: a 512px store listing, a 48px home-screen icon, and a 16–24px favicon/notification icon. If the faceted detail disappears at the smallest size, simplify the faceting rather than shrinking a detailed version down.

---

## What to avoid

- Don't literally reproduce the reference logo's arrow/flag-like shape — it belongs to someone else's brand; the brief is asking for their finish, not their form.
- No mortarboard/graduation-cap iconography, no generic open-book-with-lightbulb — both read as stock "education app" clichés and won't stand out next to the actual competition.
- No wordmark baked into the icon itself. "Matriq" as text lives next to the mark in-app and in marketing, not inside the icon — icons that try to hold a full word rarely stay legible small.
- Nothing that only works on a dark background — test the mark sitting on both a plain white home screen and a plain black one; the glow/gradient treatment needs to hold up on both.
